A Controlled Analysis of Physics-Informed Augmentation for Powder X-Ray Diffraction Classification

Kishan Paschapur, Pranav Kulkarni (Stanford) · IEEE ONCON 2026

The question. Classifiers trained on simulated X-ray diffraction patterns score well on simulated tests. Do they work on real measurements?

Approach. Before testing anything, I audited the standard setup and found two problems: a split bug that silently dropped the four most common crystal classes from training, and 285 near-duplicate structures shared between the train and test sets. With those documented, I trained the same model with and without physically realistic noise (peak broadening, background, peak shifts) and compared them on real patterns.

Top-5 accuracy on real patterns: 30% → 55%

What's next. A pre-registered journal study on a much larger set of real measurements.

Human–AI trust under information imbalance

MIT Beaver Works Summer Institute · Research Co-Lead · co-first author · built the study platform · in preparation for CHI 2027 Posters

The question. Do people lean on an AI advisor more when they can see less than it can?

The platform. A web version of the game built for the study. A fixed, scripted schedule makes the AI wrong on exactly 1 in 4 labels, so every participant sees the same accuracy. It assigns participant codes, runs per-round surveys, times each decision, keeps logs that are never overwritten, and is covered by 11 automated tests. IP-address logging is off, as the study's privacy commitments require.
