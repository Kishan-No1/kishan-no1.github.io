Dead or Alive: a platform for studying human–AI trust

MIT Beaver Works Summer Institute · Serious Games & AI · Jul 2026 – present

Problem

Do people lean on an AI advisor more when they can see less? To measure that, we needed a game where the AI's mistakes are controlled and every decision is logged, so that every participant faces the same AI.

My contribution

In the summer I led our five-person team and built the fog and vision system, the settings and mode picker, and core game logic. This fall I took over a teammate's unmerged web branch and turned it into the study platform: locked settings, a scripted error schedule, participant codes, a rules quiz, per-round surveys, decision timing measured in the browser, and logs that are never overwritten.

Hardest technical decision

How to make an AI that is "about 75% accurate" honestly. Our detector was nearly perfect, and the original build injected random misreads below a confidence threshold, so each player saw a different error rate. I replaced that with a scripted schedule: exactly 1 in 4 characters gets a wrong label that implies a different action. Every participant sees the same accuracy, and the "about 75%" on screen is true. The tradeoff is that the errors are less natural, in exchange for results that can be compared across participants.

Testing

11 automated tests, including a full round through the detector, plus manual end-to-end runs in the browser. I audited what the server logs and found it was printing client IP addresses, which our privacy commitments ruled out, so I turned that off.

What I learned

Measure before trusting the spec. A real round ends after 11–15 characters, not the 60 decisions we planned around, so the study's sample-size plan had to be redone. And read someone else's code fully before you change it.
